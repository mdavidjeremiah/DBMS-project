Hardware World
Detailed Logical Data Model and Normalization (UNF → 1NF → 2NF → 3NF)
1. Introduction
This document translates the Enhanced Entity Relationship Diagram (EERD) for Hardware World into a detailed logical (relational) model. Every entity, weak entity, associative entity, and specialization subtype from the EERD is mapped to a relation with a defined primary key (PK), foreign keys (FK), data types, and nullability. Section 2 presents the full data dictionary. Section 3 demonstrates, through three worked case studies, how each part of the schema was progressively normalized from an unnormalized (UNF) flat structure through 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF, removing repeating groups, partial dependencies, and transitive dependencies at each stage. Section 4 confirms that the final schema in Section 2 is fully in 3NF.
1.1 Mapping rules applied
Every strong entity → one relation; its identifying attribute becomes the primary key.
Every weak entity (SALE_ITEM) → a relation whose primary key is the composite of the owner's PK plus its own partial key.
Every M:N relationship with attributes (SUPPLIER↔PRODUCT) → resolved into an associative relation (SUPPLY) with a composite PK of both foreign keys.
Specialization of EMPLOYEE (disjoint, total) → one relation per subtype, each carrying EmployeeID as both PK and FK back to EMPLOYEE (the standard 'one table per subtype plus supertype' mapping).
Every 1:N relationship → a foreign key placed on the 'many' side referencing the 'one' side's primary key.
The 1:1 relationship Branch–BranchManager → a foreign key (ManagerEmployeeID) placed on BRANCH, since every branch has exactly one manager.
The recursive Employee 'supervises' relationship → a self-referencing foreign key, SupervisorID, on EMPLOYEE.

3. Normalization Walkthrough
Three representative subsystems are walked through from an unnormalized report to 3NF. Together they illustrate the three classical anomalies normalization removes: repeating groups (1NF), partial dependencies on part of a composite key (2NF), and transitive dependencies through a non-key attribute (3NF).
3.1 Case Study 1 — Sales Invoice (repeating group + partial + transitive dependencies)
Source document: a till receipt / sales report combining the sale header with every item purchased.
UNF — SALES_INVOICE (unnormalized)
SaleID, SaleDate, BranchID, BranchName, EmployeeID, EmployeeName, CustomerID, CustomerName, CustomerPhone, TotalAmount, {ItemID, ItemName, UnitPrice, Quantity} (repeating group, one occurrence per line item)
Functional dependencies observed:
SaleID → SaleDate, BranchID, EmployeeID, CustomerID, TotalAmount
BranchID → BranchName
EmployeeID → EmployeeName
CustomerID → CustomerName, CustomerPhone
ItemID → ItemName, UnitPrice
(SaleID, ItemID) → Quantity
Step 1 → 1NF: eliminate the repeating group
The item group is flattened into its own row per line item; the key becomes composite (SaleID, ItemID). Values are now atomic, but SaleID-dependent and ItemID-dependent attributes are needlessly repeated on every line.

Step 2 → 2NF: remove partial dependencies
SaleDate, BranchID…TotalAmount depend only on SaleID (not the full key), and ItemName/UnitPrice depend only on ItemID — both are partial dependencies and must be moved out.

Step 3 → 3NF: remove transitive dependencies
Within SALE, BranchID → BranchName and EmployeeID → EmployeeName and CustomerID → CustomerName, CustomerPhone are transitive (they depend on SaleID only through another non-key attribute). These are split out into their own relations, leaving only foreign keys behind.

3.2 Case Study 2 — Payroll (transitive dependency through Department and Branch)
Source document: a monthly payroll register listing each employee together with their organisational context.
UNF / 1NF — PAYROLL_REGISTER
PayrollID, EmployeeID, EmployeeName, DepartmentID, DepartmentName, BranchID, BranchName, Month, GrossPay, Deductions, NetPay — already atomic with a single-attribute key (PayrollID), so this is already in 1NF.
Functional dependencies observed:
PayrollID → EmployeeID, Month, GrossPay, Deductions, NetPay
EmployeeID → EmployeeName, DepartmentID
DepartmentID → DepartmentName, BranchID
BranchID → BranchName
Step → 2NF: automatically satisfied
Because the primary key (PayrollID) is a single attribute, no partial dependency is possible — the relation is already in 2NF.
Step → 3NF: remove transitive dependencies
PayrollID → EmployeeID → DepartmentID → DepartmentName (and → BranchID → BranchName) are chains of transitive dependency: DepartmentName and BranchName do not depend directly on PayrollID. Update anomalies would otherwise result — e.g. renaming a department would require rewriting every payroll row for every employee in it. Decompose:
Relation
Primary Key
Attributes
PAYROLL
PayrollID
EmployeeID (FK), Month, GrossPay, Deductions, NetPay
EMPLOYEE
EmployeeID
Name, DepartmentID (FK), …
DEPARTMENT
DepartmentID
DepartmentName, BranchID (FK)
BRANCH
BranchID
BranchName, …
This reproduces PAYROLL, EMPLOYEE, DEPARTMENT and BRANCH from Section 2 exactly, and eliminates the update/insertion/deletion anomalies present in the flat register.
3.3 Case Study 3 — Supplier–Product Supply (many-to-many with a repeating group)
Source document: a supplier catalogue listing every item a supplier can provide.
UNF — SUPPLIER_CATALOGUE
SupplierID, SupplierName, {ItemID, ItemName, CostPrice, LeadTimeDays} (repeating group — one supplier lists many items).
Step 1 → 1NF: eliminate the repeating group
Flattening produces one row per (supplier, item) pair, with composite key (SupplierID, ItemID).

Attributes
SUPPLY_1NF
(SupplierID, ItemID)
SupplierName, ItemName, CostPrice, LeadTimeDays
Functional dependencies:
SupplierID → SupplierName (partial — depends on only part of the key)
ItemID → ItemName (partial — depends on only part of the key)
(SupplierID, ItemID) → CostPrice, LeadTimeDays (full functional dependency)
Step 2 → 2NF: remove partial dependencies
SupplierName and ItemName are removed to their own single-key relations, leaving only the fully-dependent attributes in the link relation:

SupplierID
SupplierName, …
PRODUCT

Step → 3NF: already satisfied — no non-key attribute in any of the three relations depends on another non-key attribute, so SUPPLIER, PRODUCT, and SUPPLY are simultaneously in 3NF and BCNF.
4. Confirmation — Final Schema is in 3NF
Applying the same reasoning across every relation in Section 2 confirms the complete Hardware World logical schema is in Third Normal Form: every non-key attribute is fully functionally dependent on its whole primary key (2NF), and no non-key attribute is transitively dependent on the primary key through another non-key attribute (3NF). Because every determinant identified in Sections 2 and 3 is also a candidate key of its relation, the schema in fact satisfies Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) as well. The specialization tables (CASHIER, PROCUREMENT_OFFICER, ACCOUNTANT, HR_STAFF, BRANCH_MANAGER) and the associative/weak relations (SUPPLY, SALE_ITEM) require no further decomposition, since each holds only attributes that depend on its full key and nothing else.
